Preserving Family Stories

Family stories are a kind of inheritance, but only if they're captured. Here are practical methods for recording and saving the stories that define your family.

Start with the storytellers

Every family has a few people who carry the stories, the ones who remember the old days and love to tell them. Start there, and start soon. Their memories are the most at risk.

Ways to record stories

Audio and video interviews

The richest method. Recording a relative telling a story preserves their voice, timing, and personality, not just the facts. Prompt them with open questions and let them talk.

Written accounts

Some people express themselves better in writing. Encourage relatives to write down stories, or transcribe recorded ones so they're searchable and easy to share.

Story prompts at gatherings

Holidays and reunions are natural times to draw out stories. A few prepared questions can turn a dinner into a treasure trove.

Organizing and protecting them

  • Keep recordings and writings backed up in more than one place
  • Label everything with names, dates, and context
  • Gather them into a single shared location the family can access

Sharing across the family

Stories gain power when they're shared. A collaborative online tribute or family memory page lets relatives everywhere read, listen to, and add to the collection, so the stories keep circulating instead of sitting in a drawer.
