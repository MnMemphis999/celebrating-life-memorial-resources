Online Memorial vs. Obituary

An obituary and an online memorial serve different purposes, and many families create both. Understanding the difference helps you decide what each should do.

Platforms like TributeWell let families create a collaborative online tribute where anyone can add photos, videos, and messages from anywhere, and revisit them for years.

What an obituary is

An obituary is a short, formal announcement of a death, traditionally published in a newspaper and now often online. It typically states the essential facts: the person's name, dates, surviving family, and service details. It's concise by design and usually written once.

What an online memorial is

An online memorial is an ongoing, interactive space. Rather than announcing a death in a few paragraphs, it celebrates a life in depth, with photos, videos, stories, and contributions from many people, and it keeps growing over time.

Key differences

  • Length: An obituary is brief; a memorial is expansive.
  • Authorship: An obituary is usually written by one person; a memorial is collaborative.
  • Timeframe: An obituary is a one-time publication; a memorial lives and grows.
  • Interactivity: An obituary is read; a memorial is contributed to.
  • Media: An obituary is mostly text; a memorial holds photos, video, and audio.

Why families use both

The obituary does the important work of announcing and informing. The online memorial does the ongoing work of remembering and gathering. Many obituaries now link directly to an online memorial so readers can go deeper and contribute their own memories.
