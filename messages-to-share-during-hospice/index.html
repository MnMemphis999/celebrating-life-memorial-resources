Messages to Share During Hospice

When time is short, words carry enormous weight, and they can be the hardest to find. Here's guidance on what to say to someone in hospice, and how to say it.

The things that matter most

Hospice professionals and families often point to a few messages that bring the most peace. In various forms, they are: I love you. Thank you. I forgive you. Please forgive me. And, when it's time, it's okay to go.

Expressing love

Be specific. Instead of a general 'I love you,' name what you love: the way they always made you feel welcome, a lesson they taught you, a moment you'll never forget. Specificity turns a sentiment into a memory.

Saying thank you

Gratitude gives someone the sense that their life mattered and their efforts were seen. Thank them for concrete things, sacrifices they made, ways they shaped who you are.

Offering and asking forgiveness

If there's unfinished business, this can be the time to gently release it. Even simple words can lift a weight both people have carried.

Giving permission

Many people hold on because they worry about those they're leaving. Reassuring them that the family will be okay, that it's alright to rest, can be one of the most loving things you offer.

When words are hard

You don't have to say anything profound. Presence, touch, and familiar routines speak too. Writing a letter, or recording a message for an online tribute, can help when speaking in the moment feels impossible.
